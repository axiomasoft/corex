## Concrete residuals to reproduce

1. DatabaseIdentitySyncer reads membership before tenant callback, then returns when identity.updated_at <= users.synced_at. Membership-only blocked/regrant state can be skipped. Existing removal test explicitly bumps identity.updated_at, leaving this case uncovered. This is source-derived risk, not a reproduced runtime failure in this design.
2. It enables membership for any status other than blocked; missing/deleted/pending/non-active authority must be qualified explicitly. Profile freshness and membership authority cannot share a wall-clock guard. Management sync writes root sync_status; never bind it as a tenant root writer.
3. Same-email identity collision cannot be demonstrated by inserting two live central rows into the frozen unique email index. Test distinct UUIDs with same phone; same email across isolated account fixtures/tombstones or forged payload must reject/fail without merging or changing the existing UUID. Do not drop the approved email index to manufacture a test.
4. B34 fallback MAX(updated_at) of active memberships can reuse an earlier timestamp/key after revoke or Redis loss when old list keys survive. Qualification must prove fresh authoritative recompute and no reachable revoked entry; timestamp-hash alone is insufficient proof. The central owner resolves any normative conflict before dependent adapter dispatch.
